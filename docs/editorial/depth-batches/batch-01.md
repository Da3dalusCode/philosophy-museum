## Epistemology

### Sources consulted

- [SEP: The Analysis of Knowledge](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/knowledge-analysis/) — the scope and limits of the knowledge-analysis project, Gettier cases, epistemic luck, safety, virtue approaches, knowledge-first views, and contextualism.
- [SEP: Skepticism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/skepticism/) — Cartesian, Pyrrhonian, and Humean skeptical pressures; foundationalist, coherentist, infinitist, and contextualist responses.
- [SEP: Internalist vs. Externalist Conceptions of Epistemic Justification](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justep-intext/) — the distinct internalism/externalism debates and hybrid positions.
- [SEP: Reliabilist Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reliabilism/) and [SEP: Virtue Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-virtue/) — truth-conducive processes, standard objections, competence, character, and epistemic value.
- [SEP: Naturalism in Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-naturalized/) and [SEP: Formal Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/formal-epistemology/) — the plurality of naturalist programs, empirical cooperation, formal tools, and idealization.
- [SEP: Bayesian Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-bayesian/) and [SEP: Understanding](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/understanding/) — degrees of belief, updating, epistemic value, and differences between knowing and understanding.
- [SEP: Social Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-social/), [SEP: Epistemological Problems of Testimony](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/testimony-episprob/), and [SEP: The Social Dimensions of Scientific Knowledge](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-knowledge-social/) — testimony, expertise, peer disagreement, groups, misinformation, science, institutions, ignorance, and epistemic injustice.
- [SEP: Feminist Epistemology and Philosophy of Science](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-epistemology/) — situated knowers, authority, objectivity, exclusion, and power-sensitive criticism of inquiry.
- [SEP: Epistemology in Classical Indian Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-india/) and [SEP: Epistemology in Chinese Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-epistemology/) — pramāṇa debates and cautions against forcing distinct Chinese concepts and practices into a justified-true-belief taxonomy.

### Structural and interpretive changes

The former six-section route through Plato, Descartes, Hume, Gettier, testimony, and a reading list became ten sections: epistemic goods; plural histories; sources of cognition; testimony and disagreement; skepticism; structures of justification; post-Gettier approaches; naturalized/formal/scientific methods; power and institutions; and open questions.

Corrections and cautions include:

- justified true belief is presented as an influential later framework, not an uncontested ancient definition or universal taxonomy;
- Gettier is treated as one important episode rather than the field’s center;
- epistemology is not narrated as beginning with Descartes or as belonging only to analytic philosophy;
- Nyāya, Buddhist pramāṇa, Greek, Islamic, and Chinese materials are situated as distinct debates rather than equivalents;
- knowledge, understanding, justification, rationality, warrant, and successful inquiry are separated;
- individual responsibility is connected to testimony, expertise, science, ignorance, injustice, and institutional design.

Remaining claim-review work includes sentence-level verification of the compressed historical survey, closer source coverage for medieval Islamic/Jewish/Christian debates, durable locators for the cross-traditional paragraphs, and review of contemporary AI/testimony terminology as that literature changes.

## Ethics

### Sources consulted

- [SEP: Moral Theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-theory/) and [SEP: Metaethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/metaethics/) — the scope of ethical theorizing, theory and decision procedures, reasons, realism, antirealism, constructivism, expressivism, and motivation.
- [SEP: Virtue Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/), [SEP: Deontological Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-deontological/), and [SEP: Contractualism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/contractualism/) — internal plurality in major normative families and the contractarian/contractualist distinction.
- [SEP: Feminist Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminism-ethics/) and [SEP: Feminist Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminist-philosophy/) — care, dependency, material conditions, intersectional power, and feminist interventions across rather than beside ethics.
- [SEP: Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/), [SEP: Moral Luck](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-luck/), [SEP: Intention](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/intention/), and [SEP: Collective Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/collective-responsibility/) — agency, reasons-responsiveness, blame, intention, luck, groups, and remedial responsibility.
- [SEP: Global Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-global/), [SEP: Environmental Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/), [SEP: Philosophy of Technology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/technology/), and [SEP: Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-ai/) — institutions, global duties, environment, design, power, present technological harms, and uncertainty.
- [SEP: Chinese Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-chinese/), [SEP: Mencius](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mencius/), and [SEP: Ethics in Indian Buddhism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-indian-buddhism/) — ritual, role, cultivation, intention, suffering, discipline, compassion, and internal disagreements.
- [SEP: African Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/african-ethics/) — character, personhood, reciprocity, community, common good, and the need not to erase cultural plurality.

### Structural and interpretive changes

The former six-section overview became eleven sections organized around the field’s scope; plural histories; reasons and value; consequentialisms; duties, rights, and agreement; virtue and care; agency and luck; metaethics; institutions and global justice; applied domains; and critical method.

Corrections and cautions include:

- ethics is not organized as a contest among utilitarianism, Kantian deontology, and Aristotelian virtue ethics;
- normative ethics, metaethics, moral psychology, applied ethics, and political dimensions are distinguished without being isolated;
- Confucian, Buddhist, Jain, South Asian, African, Jewish, Christian, and Islamic materials are acknowledged as internally diverse traditions with different organizing concepts;
- care, dependency, embodiment, disability, and structural wrongdoing are treated as central rather than optional applications;
- collective and institutional responsibility are separated from indiscriminate group blame;
- AI ethics remains connected to present labor, data, power, environment, and accountability rather than only speculative machine status.

Remaining claim-review work includes more specialist sourcing for Jain, Islamic, Jewish, and contemporary African debates; sentence-level verification of historical compression; durable evidence for claims about institutional and technological ethics; and assessment of whether selected broad comparisons need narrower attribution.

## Analytic Philosophy

### Sources consulted

- [IEP: Analytic Philosophy](https://iep.utm.edu/analytic-philosophy/) — traditional origin narratives, the linguistic turn, post-linguistic plurality, and definitional difficulty.
- [SEP: Analysis](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/analysis/) and its supplement on conceptions of analysis — historical varieties of analysis, logical analysis, ordinary-language work, explication, conceptual analysis, and conceptual engineering.
- [SEP: Russell’s Logical Atomism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logical-atomism/) — logical construction, atomism, later criticism, and influence on logical positivism.
- [SEP: Vienna Circle](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vienna-circle/) and [SEP: Willard Van Orman Quine](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/quine/) — the Circle’s internal variety and political setting, analyticity, holism, naturalism, and the Carnap–Quine dispute.
- [SEP: Modal Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-modal/) and [SEP: Possible Worlds](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/possible-worlds/) — mid-century formal developments and the later metaphysical significance of modality.
- [SEP: Experimental Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/experimental-philosophy/) — empirical methods, methodological diversity, and challenges to intuition-based practice.
- Hans-Johann Glock, [*What Is Analytic Philosophy?*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511841125) — the limits of doctrinal, stylistic, geographical, and methodological definitions and a family-resemblance/tradition approach.
- Michael Beaney, ed., [*The Oxford Handbook of the History of Analytic Philosophy*](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28176) — historiography, contested origins, logic, ordinary language, modality, ethics, political philosophy, and canon formation.
- James Pearson, [review of Matt LaVine’s *Race, Gender, and the History of Early Analytic Philosophy*](https://jhaponline.org/article/view/5097/4122), *Journal for the History of Analytical Philosophy* 10(2) — evidence for contemporary debate about race, gender, canon, and sociopolitical engagement in histories of the tradition.

### Structural and interpretive changes

The former six-section origin-to-Quine narrative became twelve sections: the category problem; plural contexts and origin stories; early logical analysis; logical empiricism; later Wittgenstein and ordinary language; Quine and naturalism; modality and metaphysics; normative and social philosophy; plural methods; pragmatist/phenomenological/continental crossings; institutions and exclusions; and a non-triumphalist reading route.

Corrections and cautions include:

- no thinker is named as the uncontested founder, and origin stories are separated from retrospective canon formation;
- the Vienna Circle is not flattened into one verification principle or treated as politically neutral;
- later Wittgenstein, Austin, Ryle, and Anscombe are not reduced to dictionary work;
- Quine’s critique is not presented as a final refutation followed by one inevitable successor program;
- modal logic and renewed metaphysics are included without making them inevitable consequences of Frege or Russell;
- ethics, political philosophy, feminist philosophy, philosophy of race, and social epistemology are placed within analytic institutions;
- analytic/continental is treated as an imperfect institutional boundary, not a map of rigor, topic, politics, or geography;
- ancient and non-European analytic methods are distinguished from the specific modern Western tradition rather than appropriated into it.

Remaining claim-review work includes more primary and specialist coverage for Susan Stebbing, Neurath, postwar philosophy of mind/language, global institutional expansion, and excluded figures; sentence-level chronology checks; and review of how much weight to place on contested family-resemblance and genealogical definitions.

### Source limitations and rejected material

Search-result snippets and Wikipedia pages were used only to discover candidate sources, never as substantive evidence for the drafts. Two candidate downloadable histories of analytic philosophy could not be opened reliably in the research environment, so their contents were not used or attributed. Paywalled or preview-limited books were used only to the extent supported by accessible publisher metadata and review material; no inaccessible passage was paraphrased as though consulted. The visitor-facing bibliography therefore favors stable SEP, IEP, publisher, DOI, and journal URLs whose relevance could be checked directly.
