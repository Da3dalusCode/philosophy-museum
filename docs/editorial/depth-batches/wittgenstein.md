## Major editorial decisions

The article uses early, middle, later, and final phases as navigation aids, not separate personalities. The *Tractatus* and *Investigations* differ over general form, hidden logical analysis, grammar, and plurality of methods. Continuities remain visible in clarification, opposition to philosophy as a natural science, concern with sense, unusual literary form, ethical seriousness, and suspicion of metaphysical explanation. The middle transition carries philosophical weight: color exclusion, phenomenological notation, calculus models, grammar, the Vienna Circle, and dictated books show how the later work emerged.

The account of language blocks the requested simplifications. Meaning as use is limited to “many cases” and includes standards, circumstances, contrasts, and consequences. Language-games are activities woven into forms of life, not arbitrary sealed games. Family resemblance permits useful definition and boundaries without assuming one essence. Forms of life remain open among natural, cultural, plural, and more universal readings.

The rule-following section distinguishes Wittgenstein’s regress pressure from the claim that rules are impossible. Training, correction, practice, and going on make normativity possible without making an interpretation foundational or a community infallible. Kripke’s skeptical interpretation is presented as influential and contested rather than silently identified with Wittgenstein.

The private-language section denies neither experience nor privacy. It targets a criterion of correctness available only through an inaccessible private impression. The mind section also rejects behaviorism: expression, circumstance, history, and behavior belong to psychological grammar without identifying experience with outward motion. First- and third-person uses are allowed to be asymmetrical.

Therapy is presented as plural methods of clarification, not contempt for every theory or argument. Wittgenstein argues, objects, compares, and designs examples; the target is dogmatic philosophical explanation that ignores the grammar producing its object. Dialogue, questions, imagined voices, recurring examples, and aspect changes are treated as philosophical method rather than ornament.

*On Certainty* is presented as developing final notes, not a completed anti-skeptical system. The article preserves propositional, non-propositional, epistemic, non-epistemic, framework, contextualist, and therapeutic approaches to hinges. Radical doubt is criticized through the practice of doubting rather than answered with a new indubitable proposition.

Ethics, value, religion, and the mystical are not appended curiosities. The early saying/showing boundary makes their status central to traditional, resolute, ineffabilist, and ethical readings of the *Tractatus*. Later religious remarks are distinguished by genre and do not yield one settled fideism, noncognitivism, or realism. Personal moral severity is not substituted for an ethical theory.

The corpus section separates the published *Tractatus*, the briefly published “Some Remarks on Logical Form,” the authorized but posthumous *Investigations*, dictated books, student notes, notebooks, manuscripts, and editor-created volumes. Anscombe, Rhees, and von Wright are credited as literary heirs without assuming that editorial fidelity erased selection, order, titling, or translation. The later corpus is neither dismissed as inauthentic nor treated as if every title were Wittgenstein’s finished book.
