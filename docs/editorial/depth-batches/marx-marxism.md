## Sources consulted

### Marx primary corpus and textual status

- Marx’s *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844*, *Theses on Feuerbach*, *The German Ideology* manuscripts with Engels, *The Communist Manifesto*, *The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte*, the *Grundrisse*, *A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy*, *Capital* Volumes I–III, *The Civil War in France*, *Critique of the Gotha Program*, the Vera Zasulich drafts and letter, Civil War journalism and addresses, and selected late notebooks supplied the primary corpus.
- The [Marx/Engels Library](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/index.htm) was used as a public chronological portal. It is not treated as a substitute for checking editions, translations, authorship, or manuscript status.
- The Berlin-Brandenburg Academy’s [Marx-Engels-Gesamtausgabe](https://mega.bbaw.de/) project was consulted for the historical-critical division among authored publications, manuscripts and drafts, correspondence, excerpts, and notes. The article does not describe every late excerpt as a settled Marxian position.
- Capital Volume I is identified as Marx’s published volume. Volumes II and III are identified as Engels’s posthumous editorial constructions from Marx’s manuscripts. The *Grundrisse*, 1844 manuscripts, *German Ideology* manuscripts, and ethnological and natural-science notebooks are identified as unfinished or unpublished research materials rather than books Marx authorized in their familiar later form.

### Marx specialist references and disputed interpretation

- [SEP: Karl Marx](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/marx/) supplied a specialist map of alienation, history, economics, morality, ideology, state, communism, and major interpretive alternatives.
- *The Cambridge Companion to Marx* was used especially for the need to separate Marx’s texts from Engels’s and later interpretations, and for political theory, reproduction, gender, and nature.
- *The Oxford Handbook of Karl Marx* was used across historical materialism, class, institutions, social reproduction, crisis, and ecological debates.
- Michael Heinrich’s *An Introduction to the Three Volumes of Karl Marx’s Capital* supplied one influential monetary and value-form-oriented route through value, money, all three volumes, and their editorial architecture. Its controversial conclusions are presented as one interpretation rather than consensus.
- Kevin B. Anderson’s *Marx at the Margins* was used for Marx’s changing work on Ireland, nationalism, ethnicity, colonialism, Russia, and non-Western historical paths. The article preserves the dispute over whether late developments overcome earlier Eurocentrism.
- Heather A. Brown’s *Marx on Gender and the Family* and the Cambridge Companion chapters on reproduction and gender informed the distinction between openings in Marx and later feminist or social-reproduction theories.
- Kohei Saito’s *Marx in the Anthropocene* and scholarship represented in the Oxford Handbook informed the ecological and late-notebook debate. The article treats metabolic-rift, productivist, and degrowth readings as disputed.
- Matteo Battistini’s specialist article on Marx and the global history of the American Civil War informed the treatment of slavery, divided labor, the world market, abolition, and democracy. Marx’s contribution is neither reduced to class indifference nor inflated into a complete theory of racial formation.

### Marxism, movements, and state histories

- Primary works by Engels, Kautsky, Bernstein, Luxemburg, Lenin, Trotsky, Gramsci, Lukács, Horkheimer, Adorno, Mao, Althusser, Black and anticolonial writers, feminist theorists, analytical Marxists, dependency theorists, autonomists, and ecological Marxists were used to identify distinct revisions rather than one continuous doctrine.
- [SEP: Antonio Gramsci](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gramsci/), [Critical Theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/critical-theory/), [Analytical Marxism](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2022/entries/marxism-analytical/), and [Ideology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ideology/) supplied specialist orientation to movements with extensive internal disputes.
- *The Cambridge History of Communism* and *The Oxford Handbook of the History of Communism* supplied global histories of the Second and Third Internationals, communist parties, revolutions, Soviet and Chinese state power, anticolonial movements, and postwar transformations.
- James Harris’s *The Great Fear* was consulted for archive-based scholarship on Stalinist terror. Roderick MacFarquhar’s *The Origins of the Cultural Revolution*, the Oxford communism handbook, and Cambridge scholarship on the Great Leap Forward were consulted for Maoist state power, famine, persecution, and institutional history.
- Cedric J. Robinson’s *Black Marxism*, Du Bois’s *Black Reconstruction*, and scholarship on Black internationalism supplied distinct Black Marxist and Black radical perspectives. Robinson’s critique is not converted into a claim that every Black radical tradition is a form of Marxism.
- The SAGE and Oxford handbooks supported coverage of Black Marxism, feminist and social-reproduction debates, ecology, crisis, class analysis, and contemporary transformations.

Search-result snippets, generic ideology explainers, quotation sites, videos, party primers, and AI-generated material were not used as evidence or visitor bibliography. Publisher descriptions and accessible abstracts were used to establish scope; inaccessible arguments were not represented as fully consulted.

## Editorial decisions and major corrections

Marx is not reduced to economic determinism, a universal stage theory, the Manifesto, or later Marxism-Leninism. Historical materialism is presented as a disputed family of explanations that must connect material conditions to historically formed agency. Base-superstructure language is located in a compressed programmatic text and checked against concrete political histories and late non-Western research.

The early/late question remains open. The profile traces continuity from alienation to fetishism and domination while preserving changes in vocabulary, method, and object. Humanist and structural readings are named as disputes. The 1844 manuscripts do not dictate *Capital*, and *Capital* does not make the early problems disappear.

Value theory is not converted into the moral slogan that workers deserve the whole product. Socially necessary labor, abstract labor, money, labor-power, surplus value, transformation, prices, and profit are distinguished. Traditional labor-value, value-form, monetary, temporal, and analytical readings are presented as rival interpretations.

Marx’s political record distinguishes rights, political emancipation, concrete state analysis, the Commune, revolution, transition, and communism. His refusal of a blueprint is not treated as a solution to constitutional design. The profile makes the unresolved problems of pluralism, opposition, law, coercion, leadership, and accountability visible.

Colonialism, slavery, race, gender, and ecology are integrated into the argument rather than attached as a corrective appendix. Marx’s changes and resources are recorded together with Eurocentric language, incomplete theories of racialization and gendered reproduction, and disputes over productivism and late ecological development.

Marxism is not a second summary of Marx. It begins with editorial mediation and proceeds through intellectual arguments, organizations, movements, state power, outcomes, opposition traditions, and later reinterpretation. Marxism-Leninism is identified as a historically constructed party-state doctrine rather than the neutral definition of Marxism.

Soviet and Maoist histories are neither the whole meaning of Marxism nor irrelevant to evaluating it. Modernization, welfare, national independence, resistance, and mass participation are historically acknowledged without balancing away forced collectivization, famine, camps, executions, censorship, invasion, persecution, and party monopolies. Emergency, war, inherited institutions, doctrine, organization, leadership, and bureaucratic interests are treated as interacting explanations rather than a single exoneration or inevitable causal chain.

Black, feminist, anticolonial, social-reproduction, analytical, ecological, autonomist, democratic, religious, and contemporary traditions appear as substantive reconstructions and criticisms. Race, gender, nation, care, and ecology are not asserted to be already complete inside class analysis.

## Source limitations and later claim review

This batch is a sourced editorial synthesis, not completed claim review. Several specialist books and chapters were available through publisher metadata, abstracts, previews, or previously known editions rather than complete online text. The articles do not attribute inaccessible arguments or provide invented locators.

A later Marx claim review should map locators for corpus and publication status; alienation and species-being; the 1859 Preface; abstract labor, value, money, surplus value, transformation, profit, credit, rent, and crisis; rights, the state, the Commune, transition, and political strategy; India, Ireland, Russia, slavery, and Civil War writings; household reproduction and gender; soil, metabolism, and late natural-science notes. It should record rival interpretations rather than locking one school’s disputed reconstruction as fact.

A later Marxism claim review should separate Engels’s works and editorial interventions; Second International parties and colonial debates; Bernstein, Luxemburg, and Lenin in textual context; revolutionary, civil-war, and early Soviet institutions; Stalinist doctrine and repression; Gramsci’s prison-text history; Western Marxism and Frankfurt generations; Maoist theory, Great Leap Forward, famine, Cultural Revolution, and transnational Maoisms; anticolonial, Black, feminist, social-reproduction, analytical, autonomist, dependency, ecological, and democratic traditions. Historical outcome claims need specialist histories and appropriately precise locators. Until that work is completed, both records properly remain bibliography-only.
