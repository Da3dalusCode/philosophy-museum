## Required corrections and uncertainty decisions

1. Retain the existing disputed-date note but prefer `c. 1571/72–1635/36 (traditional 1640/41)`, medium/low confidence. S3 §§1.1 reports 1635/36 from his grandson and two paratexts, while 1640/41 is traditional without clear supporting evidence.
2. Preserve study with Mir Damad and Shaykh Baha’i, while saying no formal `ijaza` survives. S3 documents autograph/`ijaza`-like notes and rejects the apocryphal Mir Findiriski story.
3. Keep the Kahak withdrawal cautious. S3 supports a probable five-year retreat and later teaching/writing, but persecution, voluntary discipline, patronage disappointment, and a conversion-style story cannot be cleanly separated. Do not romanticize.
4. “School of Isfahan” is a later Nasr/Corbin historiographic label, not a self-declared institution. Use “Safavid philosophical networks later grouped under that label.”
5. Preserve primacy of existence, but avoid saying Mulla Sadra simply “reversed Suhrawardi’s doctrine” or that every earlier philosopher held primacy of essence. The essence/existence historiography is schematic, and Mulla Sadra’s own development requires work-specific evidence.
6. Preserve gradation/modulation of existence and mark the monism/pluralism question as central and unresolved. “Existence is one” must not erase real causal ordering, nor may “plural beings” simply neutralize his stronger unity claims.
7. Substantial motion is motion in material substance, not modern particle motion or universal instability. It supports his accounts of time and soul, but the inference to each later doctrine should be evidenced, not assumed.
8. Keep “the soul is bodily in origination and spiritual in survival” as a characteristic formula, but verify the exact *Four Journeys* psychology locator. Explain developmental embodiment without claiming a ready-made immaterial soul is inserted into the body.
9. Preserve the imaginal-resurrection account and the controversy: he argues for personal and bodily return through a transformed account of body, not reassembly of identical particles. Whether this satisfies doctrinal bodily resurrection is disputed.
10. Separate philosophical method (demonstration), illumination/intuition, Qur’anic exegesis, hadith commentary, and Ibn Arabi’s legacy. “Synthesis” means argumentative transformation, not simple harmony or eclectic compilation.
11. Reception became especially dominant in parts of Shi‘i seminary philosophy from the early nineteenth century; it was neither immediate nor universal. Preserve South Asian influence without reducing all later Islamic philosophy to Sadrianism.
12. Verify the forum object’s “1595 handwritten miscellany” against the owning collection record before treating it as biographical evidence; object date and philosophical doctrine are separate claims.
