## Method and boundary

The review covers every article paragraph and claim-bearing fact for Dignāga. It covers uncertain chronology and biography, pre-Dignāgan reasoning, the *Pramāṇasamuccaya* and its fragmentary multilingual transmission, two pramāṇas, nonconceptual perception, reflexive awareness, the three characteristics of a reason, inference for another, apoha, Yogācāra classification, Dharmakīrti’s revisions, rival criticism, reception, and staged reading. Museum and other reused surfaces were reconciled separately in the same release and remain outside this article lock.

## Sources and locator inspection

The Austrian Academy’s EAST dossier and Ernst Steinkellner’s explicitly hypothetical reconstruction of *Pramāṇasamuccaya* chapter 1 anchor the primary and philological evidence. Stephen Phillips’s SEP article supplies comparative pramāṇa and inference context; Brendan Gillon’s SEP article supplies the history of Indian logic and textual transmission; Tom Tillemans’s current Dharmakīrti SEP article distinguishes Dignāga’s architecture from later revisions; Christian Coseru’s SEP article supplies Buddhist philosophy-of-mind context. The Columbia *Apoha* volume records multiple scholarly reconstructions instead of one artificial consensus.

## Corrections and qualifications

- The conventional 480–540 range is marked low-confidence rather than documentary.
- Traditional birthplace, teacher, travel, and monastery claims do not become a precise itinerary.
- “Founder of Buddhist logic” is replaced by “foundational systematizer”; earlier Buddhist and non-Buddhist reasoning remains visible.
- “Buddhist Epistemological School” is treated as a retrospective convenience without a surviving equivalent Sanskrit institutional name.
- The complete Sanskrit *Pramāṇasamuccaya* is not represented as extant; fragments, two Tibetan translations, quotations, and commentary are named.
- Perception, inference, testimony, justification, and reflexive awareness remain distinct.
- Apoha is presented as an evolving response to the universals problem, not solved by the slogan “not non-cow.”
- Dharmakīrti’s causal and justificatory innovations are not silently attributed to Dignāga.

## Open questions and integration assumptions

Biography, chronology, metaphysical commitment, details of textual reconstruction, and the exact relation between perception and conceptual thought remain disputed. The integrated record preserves those qualifications and diacritics; the stored deterministic lock reflects its final reviewed snapshot.
