## Method and evidence

## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- The Bordeaux parlement is identified as a judicial-administrative institution, not a modern legislature.
- Persian voices are literary devices; Usbek’s domestic despotism and Roxana’s resistance prevent treating him as a transparent authorial spokesman.
- The English constitution is an idealized analysis in context, not a universal three-box diagram.
- Climate influence is distinguished from strict determinism while its obsolete physiology and Eurocentric stereotypes remain explicit.
- Commerce may moderate manners but can also price relations and support monopoly or imperial force.
- Montesquieu condemns and mocks defenses of slavery, then compromises that critique through climatic and despotic exceptions; neither “pro-slavery” nor “abolitionist” alone is adequate.
- Rome and empire are treated through the transformation of institutions by expansion, not as a simple ancient model.
