## Corrections and qualifications

- The familiar 600–660 range is not presented as settled; earlier sixth-century proposals and agnosticism remain visible.
- South Indian origin, Mīmāṃsā formation, Nālandā, Dharmapāla, and personality stories are attributed to traditional or relational sources.
- Dharmakīrti is a major reviser, not merely Dignāga’s expositor.
- Reliable cognition requires an appropriate causal or inferential route; practical success alone is insufficient.
- Truth, justification, ascertainment, reflexive awareness, and successful activity are not collapsed.
- Momentariness is a technical causal thesis rather than the truism that things change.
- Non-deviation and causal connection are kept distinct from a modern possible-world necessity claim.
- Scripture is conditional and fallible, not an independent infallible pramāṇa.
- Tibetan doctrinal taxonomies are receptions rather than direct quotations from the Indian author.
