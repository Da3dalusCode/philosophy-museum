## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- Pragmatism is tied to conceivable practical bearings in disciplined inquiry, not expediency or whatever produces an immediate payoff.
- Inquiry begins from genuine disruption of settled habit; paper doubt is not assigned the same role.
- The long-run ideal is a normative picture of indefinitely self-correcting inquiry, not a prediction that present consensus must be true.
- Abduction is distinguished from deduction and induction, and its formulation is allowed to develop across Peirce’s career.
- Sign classifications are described as evolving rather than frozen into one simple triad.
- Peirce is a major innovator in logic and semiotics without being declared the sole inventor of modern logic.
