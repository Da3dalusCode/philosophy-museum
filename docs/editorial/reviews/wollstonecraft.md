## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- Rights and education are joined as conditions of independent virtue rather than reduced to schooling alone.
- Traits used to justify women’s subordination are explained as products and strategies of dependence.
- Her criticism of Rousseau is placed inside a wider argument about civic education, marriage, work, and national reform.
- Marriage is criticized as property and mastery while reciprocal family friendship remains a positive aim.
- Middling-class ideals, servants, labor, property, commercial improvement, and acquisitiveness preserve class tensions.
- Universal rights supply anti-domination resources, but civilizational vocabulary and analogies do not become a fully modern anti-imperial theory.
- Reviews, fiction, aesthetics, and travel are philosophical genres, not decorative biography.
- *Maria* is clearly marked unfinished and posthumous; later feminist significance is explained without projecting a complete present-day category backward.
