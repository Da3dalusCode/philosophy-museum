## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- The broad early use of “paradigm” is clarified through disciplinary matrices and exemplars rather than repeated as one univocal technical term.
- An isolated anomaly does not automatically produce crisis or revolution; historical judgment, a research community, and a viable alternative matter.
- Incommensurability is not absolute mutual unintelligibility, and Kuhn’s later taxonomic emphasis is distinguished from stronger reception claims.
- Shared theory-choice values constrain judgment without functioning as an algorithm and without reducing scientific choice to arbitrary politics.
- Kuhn does not say that communities simply invent facts, nor does he deny every form of progress; his non-cumulative account of progress and realism remains disputed.
- Relations to Popper and logical empiricism are treated as arguments and partial overlaps, not a one-event defeat narrative.
