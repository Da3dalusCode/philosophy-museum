## Required corrections and uncertainty decisions

1. Keep `1126–1198` as secure conventional anchors and use “Ibn Rushd (Averroes)” at first mention. Biography should not make Ibn Tufayl a documented teacher or assume why the Almohad exile occurred; philosophical teachers and the political episode are incompletely known.
2. Qualify the neat “short, middle, and long commentaries” taxonomy. R3 notes that the threefold classification is a modern scholarly convention increasingly challenged because the corpus also includes compendia, questions, treatises, and revisions, while chronological relations can be complex. It remains a useful orientation, not a universal authorial scheme.
3. The *Decisive Treatise* is a juridical inquiry into what revealed Law requires or permits for qualified people, not a secular manifesto for reason against religion. Preserve its differentiated audiences and rules for demonstrative error and interpretation.
4. Explicitly reject the “double truth” caricature. “Truth does not oppose truth” in R1 grounds the claim that sound demonstration and revelation cannot ultimately conflict. Later Latin “Averroism” and condemnations are reception categories, not transparent descriptions of Ibn Rushd’s doctrine.
5. Rewrite intellect claims as developmental. The mature R2 III.5 and III.17–18 posit one separate material intellect and agent intellect in a difficult account of individual human understanding, but earlier short/middle treatments differ. Do not compress this into “Averroes believed everyone shares one mind.”
6. Qualify the “commentary on Plato’s *Republic*.” It is a paraphrase/commentary surviving principally in Hebrew, and the exact Greek/Arabic textual source available to him is disputed. Do not imply access to a complete direct Greek text.
7. Replace “purified Aristotle of Avicennian distortions” with “criticized many Avicennian departures while also engaging and sometimes accommodating Avicennian problems.” His anti-Avicennian rhetoric is real; total doctrinal independence is not.
8. Correct the medical portrait. He wrote the *Kulliyyat* on general medical principles, but R3 notes medicine was not simply his principal profession and reports his own limited practical experience. Avoid turning court-physician status into a modern clinical biography.
9. Preserve uneven Arabic reception but reject the myth of no Islamic afterlife. Distinguish Arabic/Hebrew transmission, later Islamic readers, Latin translations, and the contested historiographic category “Latin Averroism.”
10. Make the 1175 timeline date approximate and work-specific. “Averroes comments on Aristotle” describes a decades-long, revised project, not a one-year event.
