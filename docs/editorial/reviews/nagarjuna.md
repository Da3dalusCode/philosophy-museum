## Claims retained

The review retained Nāgārjuna’s central importance for Madhyamaka, the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* as the core secure work, the critique of intrinsic nature (`svabhāva`), the relation between emptiness and dependent arising, the use of reductive and dialectical argument, the importance of the two truths, and the large Indian, Tibetan, East Asian, and modern interpretive reception.

## Corrections and qualifications

- The lifespan is presented as approximately 150–250 CE, with sparse historical evidence and legendary biography explicitly separated.
- South Indian association is presented cautiously rather than as a precise birthplace or court biography.
- The large traditional corpus is not treated as uniformly authentic; attribution confidence varies, and the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* is the secure center.
- `Śūnyatā` and `svabhāva` are transliterated and discussed through multiple English renderings rather than treated as transparent equivalents.
- Emptiness is not defined as nonexistence or nihilism; the page explains why Madhyamaka critics and interpreters nevertheless dispute its ontological and semantic force.
- The two truths are not described as two separate realities, and their explanatory role is identified as a major interpretive dispute.
- Causal conditions, dependence, conceptual designation, and the rejection of self-establishing essence are distinguished instead of compressed into one slogan.
- Later Madhyamaka classifications and Tibetan doxographies are presented as reception frameworks, not as categories Nāgārjuna himself explicitly authored.
- Museum key-idea labels were reconciled so “Emptiness” is not duplicated, and the critique of `svabhāva` appears as a distinct idea.

## Quotations, disputes, and unresolved questions

The profile avoids unlocated translated verse as if there were one neutral English wording. When a verse or chapter is invoked, the citation identifies the translation and standard chapter/verse division. Major unresolved questions include the historical biography, the boundaries of the authentic corpus, how to characterize Madhyamaka’s commitments without imposing an alien metaphysical scheme, the exact relation of conventional truth to language and practice, and how different Indian, Tibetan, and East Asian traditions transform the arguments.
