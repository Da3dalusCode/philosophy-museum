## Editorial decision

The article is conceptually strong and already counters the inherited equation of Philosophy of Religion with arguments about a perfect creator. It needs precision, not expansion. The revised field should include argument and conceptual analysis, but also interpretation, phenomenological description, genealogy, comparison, testimony, ritual, material practice, authority, disagreement, and critique. It should distinguish first-order assessment of claims from second-order scrutiny of the category “religion.” Traditions and methods must be named through bounded examples, not recruited as tokens or treated as conclusions.

The current base branch record is unacceptable for a locked plural article. It defines the field as faith/reason/divinity/evil/experience, asks only whether religious claims can be rationally assessed, and names four figures from Christian/Islamic theistic debates. The Museum’s canonical question and plural interpretive asset are much better, but the primary interpretation is generic and its three-source pool—SEP, IEP, Hume—overweights a general encyclopedia and a European critic. Canonical article, structured branch data, and Museum copy must converge on plurality without claiming that all traditions are interchangeable.

### Exact replacement copy for the highest-risk passages

**`overview`, paragraph 1**

> Philosophy of Religion examines arguments, concepts, practices, experiences, texts, institutions, and forms of life that are identified—often contestably—as religious or as concerning divinity, ultimacy, liberation, sacred order, or their critique. Its methods include conceptual analysis and argument, but also interpretation, phenomenological description, genealogy, comparison, and reflection on testimony and practice. Philosophers may work within a tradition, compare traditions, defend naturalism, suspend judgment, or criticize religious and secular authority. The field is therefore neither theology viewed from nowhere nor a tribunal in which one generic “religion” stands trial.

**`key-concepts`, faith/reason paragraph**

> Faith and reason name several possible relations, not two uniform faculties. Faith may involve trust, loyalty, hope, practical orientation, testimony, or assent; reason may involve demonstration, probabilistic inference, interpretation, or cultivated judgment. Much inquiry—religious and nonreligious—depends on testimony, training, inherited concepts, and trust, but those dependencies do not make all commitments equally warranted. The philosophical task is to ask which sources and practices are being trusted, what defeaters apply, how public and tradition-specific reasons interact, and when formation enables or distorts judgment.

**`internal-debates`, epistemology paragraph**

> Evidentialists ask whether religious belief is proportioned to adequate evidence. Reformed epistemologists argue that some beliefs about God may be rationally basic under suitable cognitive and social conditions. Other accounts emphasize testimony, revelation, tradition, experience, or the practical commitments through which inquiry becomes possible; fideist and existential approaches question whether detached proof captures religious trust. Each view faces objections about circularity, defeaters, harmful authority, and deep disagreement. Responses to diversity include exclusivist, inclusivist, pluralist, skeptical, and revisionary positions, but even identifying one “tradition” or one shared proposition is theory-laden. Disagreement supports humility only through further argument; it does not mechanically settle the issue.

**`reading-path`, paragraph 2**

> Next read Hume’s *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*, especially Parts II, V, and X–XI, as a staged exchange whose speakers test design, anthropomorphism, and evil rather than as a single-author verdict. Then compare one question whose aims differ: Nāgārjuna’s *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* chapter 24 joins emptiness and dependent arising to a Buddhist liberative argument, while *Analects* 3 and 17 connect ritual, spirits, and cultivated conduct without centering a creator-proof. Specialist introductions are essential. The comparison asks how arguments function within practices and ends; it does not rename Buddhism or Ru traditions versions of European theism.
