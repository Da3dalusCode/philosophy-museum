## Required corrections and uncertainty decisions

1. Replace the precise `801–873` with `c. 800–after 866 (probably early 870s)` and set low date confidence. K3 reports only an approximate birth and a death after the last securely datable evidence. Do not turn a conventional 873 into a secure fact.
2. Replace “the first philosopher of the Arabs” as an unqualified identity with “the first self-identified philosopher in the Arabic philosophical tradition.” The traditional epithet also trades on his Kindah lineage; it does not establish that speculative reasoning began with him.
3. Replace direct language that he “coordinated translators” with “worked within and probably helped patronize or direct the network conventionally called the al-Kindi circle.” K4 makes the Greek–Syriac–Arabic mediation secure; individual division of labor is reconstructive.
4. State that *On First Philosophy* survives incompletely—only the first part, in four sections—not as a complete metaphysical system. Hundreds of titles are attributed to him, but much of the corpus is lost and authenticity varies.
5. Correct the intellect précis. K1/K3 distinguish an external “first intellect” from human intellect in potentiality, actuality, and acquired/manifest use; the terms are not yet the same system later found in al-Farabi or Ibn Rushd.
6. Keep the scientific breadth but separate secure extant work, title-list attribution, and modern reconstruction. Astrology must not be presented as presently validated science. Cryptanalysis may be described as an early, and apparently the oldest surviving extended, use of frequency analysis—not as the invention of cryptography as such.
7. Replace the long list of later named thinkers as if direct personal influence were established. Separate: (a) formation of Arabic philosophical vocabulary and a translation network; (b) later Arabic engagement; and (c) Latin transmission of particular works in optics, intellect, astrology, and sciences. Aquinas should not be placed in a simple direct chain.
8. Preserve the account of finite creation, divine unity, and negative predication, but avoid making al-Kindi’s synthesis either straightforwardly Aristotelian or a settled kalam affiliation. The pseudo-Aristotelian Neoplatonic inheritance is essential.
