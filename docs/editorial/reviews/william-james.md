## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- James’s psychology laboratory is not called the uncontested first laboratory of its kind.
- Pragmatic truth is not “whatever is useful”: experiential resistance, coherence with other truths, consequences, and correction remain part of the account.
- “The Will to Believe” is limited to live, forced, momentous options not decidable in advance; it is not a license for wishful belief generally.
- Radical empiricism, pure experience, and the stream of consciousness are distinguished rather than treated as one doctrine or James’s sole discoveries.
- The James–Lange theory and the interpretation of religious experience remain contested; *Varieties* is not treated as a proof of theology.
- Participation in Agassiz’s Brazilian expedition is documented as involvement in a racialized scientific institution but is not used alone to infer endorsement of all Agassiz’s racial conclusions.
