## Material weaknesses corrected

- Replaced a generic profile with an article situated in Cappadocian family, episcopal, conciliar, ascetic, and polemical contexts.
- Connected divine incomprehensibility to the specific anti-Eunomian problem of names rather than presenting free-floating mysticism.
- Made created mutability a condition of both moral danger and unlimited growth, not a synonym for defect.
- Treated body-soul unity, resurrection, ancient physiology, sex, and gender as interpretively difficult rather than praising disembodied ascent.
- Explained perpetual progress through the infinity of the good while retaining scripture, community, virtue, and descent in service.
- Included the social force and historical limits of Gregory’s critique of slavery and economic domination.
- Presented universal restoration as strongly textually grounded and genuinely disputed rather than forcing a confessional verdict.

## Uncertainties retained

- Birth and death years, education, work chronology, and the institutional weight of Gregory’s post-381 role remain approximate.
- *On the Soul and the Resurrection* is treated as a crafted dialogue that preserves Macrina’s importance without claiming a transcript.
- The relation between universal human nature and embodied individual persons remains philosophically and developmentally contested.
- The scope of restoration, its relation to freedom and punishment, and the interpretation of non-universal-sounding passages remain open.
- Later Byzantine, mystical, and Palamite receptions are not substituted for Gregory’s own fourth-century vocabulary.
