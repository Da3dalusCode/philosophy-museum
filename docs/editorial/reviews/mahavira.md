## Method and boundaries

The profile replaces a compressed founder biography with an evidence-layered article and structured record. Every section is mapped to a named primary witness and/or specialist secondary source. Review covered the article, classification, date label and note, biography, works, ideas, branch roles, disputes, and reading paths. It distinguishes historical inference, Jain sacred history, Śvetāmbara transmission, Digambara disagreement, and later philosophical systematization. Museum, timeline, and other reuse surfaces were reconciled separately in the same entity-complete release and are not part of this deterministic article lock.

## Sources and locator inspection

Primary evidence uses Jacobi’s historical translation of the Śvetāmbara *Ācārāṅga Sūtra*, cited by book, lecture, and lesson, plus Tatia’s translation of the *Tattvārtha Sūtra* for later systematic doctrine. Neither is treated as a verbatim transcript of the historical Mahāvīra. The independent secondary controls are Paul Dundas’s *The Jains*, Padmanabh Jaini’s *The Jaina Path of Purification*, Marie-Hélène Gorisse’s SEP *Jaina Philosophy*, and Jasmine Kelly’s institutional JAINpedia article. Publisher or institutional records were inspected for titles, editions, and source roles.

## Corrections and qualifications

- The Śvetāmbara 599–527 BCE and Digambara 582–510 BCE chronologies are traditional, not documentary dates; modern reconstructions remain dependent on the disputed Buddha chronology.
- Early Buddhist references to Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta count as external attestation to a teacher and community, not neutral biography or proof of every Jain teaching.
- Mahāvīra is the twenty-fourth Jina in Jain sacred history and a reformer or renewer of an earlier nirgrantha milieu, not a clean modern founder.
- Pārśva is presented as the plausible earlier lineage figure; the first twenty-two Tīrthaṃkaras are not assigned recoverable historical dates.
- Śvetāmbara embryo transfer, marriage, clothing, and canonical claims are attributed; conflicting Digambara positions are not silently harmonized.
- Anekāntavāda, naya, and syādvāda are identified as later technical developments in Mahāvīra’s authoritative lineage, not complete systems demonstrably formulated by him.
- Ahiṃsā is explained through a densely living world, graded vows, unavoidable embodied harm, and karmic purification rather than a modern tolerance slogan.

## Remaining uncertainties and integration acceptance

The exact life chronology, relationship to Pārśva’s historical community, earliest recoverable doctrinal layer, and reconstruction of the first community remain unresolved. The integrated record preserves these qualifications, and its lock was generated only after the article and structured metadata stabilized.
