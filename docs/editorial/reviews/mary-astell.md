## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- Women’s apparent inferiority is explained through denied education and custom against a background of rational-soul equality.
- The proposed female retirement is a religious-educational institution, refuge, and friendship community, not simply a modern secular university.
- Rational method includes attention, definition, ordered ideas, testimony, belief, and disciplined judgment rather than a slogan about “reason over emotion.”
- Freedom is rational self-command under conditions that can be distorted by fashion, flattery, ignorance, and dependence.
- Formal marital consent is distinguished from adequately informed and socially uncoerced choice.
- Astell criticizes arbitrary husbands and the marriage/slavery analogy without rejecting marriage, authorizing divorce, or granting wives a general right of resistance.
- High Church Anglicanism, Tory politics, and passive obedience are constitutive commitments and generate a real tension with anti-domination arguments.
- “Early feminist” is used as a qualified retrospective classification, not as a claim that Astell supplied a complete modern feminist platform.
- The genteel class scope of the institutional proposal is stated rather than universalized.
