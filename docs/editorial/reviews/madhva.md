## Method and boundaries

The review covers the article and structured chronology, corpus, independent/dependent ontology, five differences, epistemology, hermeneutics, self, liberation, hierarchy, polemics, later transmission, and reading fields. It distinguishes Madhva’s terse corpus from early sacred biography and from the more systematic arguments of Jayatīrtha, Vyāsatīrtha, and later Mādhva authors. Institutional and devotional history is included without assuming that every received Uḍupi arrangement or Haridāsa formulation existed unchanged in Madhva’s lifetime. Museum and other reused surfaces were reconciled separately in the same release and remain outside this article lock.

The count includes article paragraph prose only and excludes titles, metadata, sources, citations, and this note.

## Evidence base

Valerie Stoker’s IEP article supplies the specialist overview of difference, dependence, knowledge, self, and liberation. Deepak Sarma’s monograph and translated appendices provide a secondary reconstruction paired with named primary selections. Jonathan Peterson’s “Before the Reign of Realism” and Valerie Stoker’s “In Charisma’s Wake” control early intellectual, institutional, hagiographic, and sacred-geographic claims. Michael Williams’s open 2024 monograph supplies independent chronology cautions and the later technical history of Mādhva realism and Vyāsatīrtha.

## Corrections and qualifications

- The widely used 1238–1317 dates are low-confidence; 1199–1278 and other chronologies are acknowledged.
- The *Sumadhvavijaya* is read as early sacred biography rather than transparent modern documentation.
- Dvaita is described as a plural realist system structured by one independent and many dependent realities, not merely two substances.
- Pañcabheda is fully specified while not allowed to exhaust the tradition.
- Perception, inference, and testimony are coordinated; Dvaita is not reduced to scriptural assertion or “literalism.”
- The popular negative-particle anecdote does not stand in for Madhva’s wider hermeneutics, and unusual etymological/textual practices remain open to criticism.
- Tāratamya and differentiated destinies are presented directly with the problem of divine justice; they are neither hidden nor made the tradition’s sole teaching.
- Later Jayatīrtha and Vyāsatīrtha arguments are not silently attributed to Madhva.
- Uḍupi lineages and Kannada devotional receptions are treated as layered histories rather than passive translations of a founder.
