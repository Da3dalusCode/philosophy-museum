## Corrections and qualifications

- The three Critiques now answer connected problems rather than one undifferentiated problem.
- The *Prolegomena*'s analytic or regressive route is distinguished from the first Critique's progressive systematic exposition.
- Causal succession is stated as succession according to a rule; the A-Deduction's syntheses are distinguished from the separate Schematism chapter.
- Critical limits do not simply abolish metaphysics: Kant retains constrained metaphysics of nature and morals.
- Duty is not emotional coldness; the *Doctrine of Virtue*'s cultivation of sympathetic feeling is represented.
- Rational-capacity accounts now expose disputes about infants, animals, and profound cognitive disability rather than assuming uncontested standing.
- The highest good, postulates, and moral faith remain interpretively disputed.
- The Religion censorship chronology is precise; aesthetics, teleology, and philosophy-of-science claims are narrowed to what the evidence supports.
- Republicanism is distinguished from Kant's criticism of direct democracy; federation and hospitality are not inflated into a coercive world state or unlimited residence right.
- Race, gender, colonial, and disability exclusions are explicit. Kleingeld's late-reform interpretation and Lu-Adler's continuity interpretation remain unresolved.
- Kant is a precursor to German Idealism, not a member of the later movement; Schopenhauer's appropriation is identified as a transformation Kant would reject.
- The retrospective deontology relationship is `central`, not a founder claim.
- Direct Kant summaries in the 1781 timeline event and primary Museum interpretation were reconciled with the reviewed distinctions.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

The page leaves open the best one-object/two-object or aspect interpretation of transcendental idealism; conceptualist and nonconceptualist readings of intuition; the unity and practical yield of the categorical-imperative formulations; the status of freedom and the postulates; the reach of teleological judgment; whether Kant abandoned, partly revised, or retained racial hierarchy; and whether rational-capacity language can be reconstructed without excluding people with profound cognitive disabilities. No visitor-facing exact quotation was added without a durable primary locator.
