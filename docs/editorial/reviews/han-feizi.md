## Corrections and qualifications accepted

- Kept the conventional `c. 280–233 BCE` chronology while identifying the birth date and most life details as later reconstruction.
- Described the *Han Feizi* as a complete 55-chapter transmission with debated single authorship, not one securely unified autograph book.
- Qualified “Legalism” and “school” as retrospective; used “fa tradition” where the analysis requires a less misleading category.
- Refused a one-word translation of `fa`: law, standard, model, method, norm, and institution remain context-dependent possibilities.
- Distinguished `fa`, `shu`, and `shi`, then showed why chapter 40 prevents treating positional power as mechanically sufficient or the triad as a simple additive formula.
- Distinguished public fa from ruler-controlled and partly secret shu; highlighted the resulting information and accountability paradox.
- Limited the psychology claim to recurring political assumptions about advantage, harm, and role conflict rather than attributing Xunzi’s complete theory of bad human nature to Han Fei.
- Treated study under Xunzi as later testimony and intellectual relationship, while preserving their disagreements about ritual, learning, moral cultivation, and exemplary persons.
- Explicitly identified authoritarian commitments, censorship, punishment, and unconstrained sovereignty instead of recasting the text as neutral management advice.

## Uncertainties retained

The exact authorship and date of individual chapters remain unsettled, especially where genres and doctrines diverge. The relation of the Laozi commentaries to the historical Han Fei is disputed. The degree of intellectual dependence on Xunzi, Shang Yang, Shen Buhai, and Shen Dao cannot be reduced to a clean teacher-to-system genealogy. Modern comparisons to rule of law, realism, principal-agent theory, and management remain limited analogies rather than historical identities.
