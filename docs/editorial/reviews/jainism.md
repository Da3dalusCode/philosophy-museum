## Corrections and qualifications

- Jainism has no one founder in its own cosmic account; the historical page uses an ancient nirgrantha community and Mahāvīra’s reform as recoverable anchors.
- The twenty-four-Jina lineage is sacred history. Pārśva receives cautious historical plausibility; the earlier Jinas do not receive invented dates.
- The Śvetāmbara canon and Digambara loss account are stated separately. The surviving *Ācārāṅga* never stands for all Jain scripture.
- Karma is subtle matter bound to plural active selves, not merely a metaphor for moral consequences.
- “Atheistic” is replaced by the more precise rejection of a creator alongside gods, liberated exemplars, cosmology, devotion, and ritual.
- Anekāntavāda is realist many-sidedness, and syādvāda is disciplined conditional predication; neither licenses “all opinions are true.”
- Mendicant great vows and household limited vows explain graded responsibility without promising materially harmless embodiment.
- Women’s large participation, Śvetāmbara and Digambara disagreements over liberation, clothing, scripture, and image practice, and internal subtraditions remain visible.
