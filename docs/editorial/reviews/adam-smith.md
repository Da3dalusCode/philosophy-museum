## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- Sympathy is imaginative fellow-feeling, not benevolence; self-interest is one situated motive, not Smith’s complete psychology.
- The impartial spectator is a disciplined standpoint, not public opinion or an infallible inner oracle.
- Justice’s strictness, resentment, virtue, and historically developing jurisprudence remain connected.
- Labor productivity is presented with bargaining inequality, class interests, cognitive damage, and public education.
- Natural liberty operates through law and institutions; the invisible hand is not a universal welfare theorem.
- Smith’s critique of mercantilism and colonial monopoly is preserved alongside his proposal for a reformed imperial federation and incomplete treatment of slavery.
- Defense, justice, public works, education, and taxation prevent a laissez-faire or stateless reading.
- The “two Smiths” problem is rejected without forcing perfect harmony across the books.
