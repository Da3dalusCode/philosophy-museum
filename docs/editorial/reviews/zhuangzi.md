## Claims retained

The review retains Zhuang Zhou as a probable later-fourth-century BCE persona; the received *Zhuangzi* as a 33-chapter composite recension divided into seven Inner, fifteen Outer, and eleven Miscellaneous chapters; Guo Xiang as a decisive editor and commentator in that received history; and perspective, language, wandering, trained skill, transformation, usefulness, embodied difference, death, and political refusal as major concerns of named chapters and the collection.

## Corrections and qualifications

- The person and the book are separated throughout. “Zhuangzi believed” is avoided when a claim belongs only to a later or uncertain textual layer.
- The dates 369–286 BCE are not presented as a secure lifespan. The profile uses a broad floruit and marks biographical anecdotes as literary or later evidence.
- Inner Chapters are traditionally closest to Zhuang Zhou but are not treated as a signed authorial manuscript. Outer and Miscellaneous materials are neither discarded nor homogenized.
- Guo Xiang’s Inner/Outer/Miscellaneous arrangement is a received textual structure, not proof of exact authorship or chronology.
- Perspectivism is distinguished from the flat claim that every belief is equally true. Skeptical, relativist, pluralist, mystical, therapeutic, ironic, and linguistic reconstructions remain in explicit debate.
- `Dao`, `shi-fei`, `tian`, `ziran`, and `wuwei` are translation-sensitive. Laozi vocabulary is not presumed to function identically in Zhuangzi contexts.
- Cook Ding models trained, cautious, locally responsive expertise, not magical effortlessness, anti-intellectual impulse, or universal infallibility.
- Butterfly and death narratives are philosophical-literary provocations, not documentary biography or a command that bereaved people suppress grief.
- Disability and bodily-difference episodes can reverse social judgments while still requiring care because an ancient text sometimes uses marked bodies for rhetorical surprise.
- Political withdrawal is presented as resistance to court capture and as a possible failure of responsibility; the tension remains open.
- Later Daoist and Chan receptions are described as transformations, not proof that the earliest text belonged to a fixed modern school or already taught Buddhist doctrine.

## Translation, quotation, and unresolved questions
