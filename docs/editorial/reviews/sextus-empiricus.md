## Claims retained and qualifications

- Sextus is presented as the principal surviving author of Pyrrhonian skepticism, while his own chronology and location remain uncertain.
- The article preserves the difference between continued investigation, suspension, and the negative doctrine that truth cannot be discovered.
- PH I’s skeptical ability, equipollence, modes, phrases, tranquility, and ordinary-life guidance are cited as distinct textual materials.
- The article states that M I–VI and M VII–XI are conventionally grouped but not simply one homogeneous work.
- Ancient medical affiliation is not treated as a settled explanatory key: Sextus’ remarks on Empiricism and Methodism remain a puzzle.
- Skeptical use of an opponent’s premises is not counted as Sextus’ personal endorsement; his reports of earlier philosophy are valuable but interested evidence.
- The article keeps ordinary-belief, practical-action, and self-refutation questions open as scholarly disputes rather than declaring Pyrrhonism either incoherent or decisively vindicated.
- Renaissance and early-modern readers are described as transforming, not merely reproducing, Sextan arguments.
