## Findings and corrections

- The conventional 551–479 BCE lifespan is labeled traditional and separated from modern documentary precision.
- Sparse recoverable evidence is distinguished from Sima Qian’s later, morally shaped career narrative.
- The *Analects* is treated as a layered collection formed and transmitted through communities, not a verbatim book authored or dictated by Confucius.
- Variant early recensions, broader circulation of Confucius sayings, received-text stabilization, and translation choices are made visible.
- `Ren` is not reduced to benevolence, `li` to etiquette, `junzi` to hereditary nobility, or `tian` to either a modern creator deity or secular nature.
- Family loyalty is examined with remonstrance, law, hierarchy, and abuse in view rather than celebrated without qualification.
- Government by virtue is not represented as a complete rejection of law or a modern democratic constitution.
- Later Confucianism and global “Confucius” images are reception histories, not a single doctrine already complete in the historical teacher.

## Uncertainties retained

Unresolved questions include the exact formation and relative dating of *Analects* strata; which sayings best preserve a historical voice; the reliability of specific official and travel episodes; the semantic range of central terms; and how far resources internal to relational and ritual ethics can criticize entrenched hierarchy. No fabricated quotation, page number, image claim, or exact event date was introduced.
