## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- Dewey’s move from idealism to experimental naturalism is presented as a development, not as a single conversion caused only by Darwin.
- Inquiry is not reduced to a rigid five-step formula, and “learning by doing” is not allowed to imply unstructured activity without reflection or curriculum.
- The Chicago Laboratory School is described as collaborative work involving teachers, administrators, and Alice Chipman Dewey.
- Democracy extends beyond elections through communication and associated life, while rights, unequal power, exclusion, and participation remain tests rather than assumed solutions.
- Jane Addams is treated as an independent theorist and reformer in a shared milieu, not merely an application of Dewey.
- Science and technology are instruments of public inquiry rather than automatic technocratic authorities; Dewey’s aesthetics is not reduced to hostility toward museums.
