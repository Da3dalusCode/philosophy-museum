## Corrections and qualifications

- Marx's writings are separated by genre, date, collaboration, manuscript status, and posthumous editing rather than presented as one finished system.
- The 1844 manuscripts, *German Ideology*, *Grundrisse*, and later *Capital* volumes retain their publication and editorial histories.
- “Historical materialism” is marked as a later label; the familiar mode-of-production scheme is not projected unchanged across the corpus.
- Alienation and mature political economy are neither collapsed into continuity nor forced into a simple epistemological break.
- Value, exploitation, realization, crisis, and the relation between values and prices are presented as live interpretive and economic disputes.
- Political emancipation and human emancipation are distinguished; the antisemitic rhetoric in *On the Jewish Question* is acknowledged rather than normalized by context.
- The dictatorship of the proletariat does not supply a complete institutional design, and the Paris Commune does not resolve pluralism, rights, or transition.
- Marx's accounts of colonialism, slavery, race, Ireland, and non-European development are presented as changing and contested rather than uniformly progressive or anticolonial.
- Gender and social reproduction are not credited to Marx at the depth developed by later feminist Marxists.
- Ecological reconstruction is balanced against productivist tensions; no simple “Marx invented environmentalism” claim remains.
- The Marxism branch relation is `precursor`, not a claim that Marx founded every later Marxist doctrine or institution.
- The 1848 timeline item now identifies the *Manifesto* as a collaborative programmatic intervention rather than a complete theory of historical materialism.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

The page leaves open continuity between young and mature Marx; the status of justice and moral criticism; the labor theory of value and exploitation; the reach of functional or technological explanation; whether later multilinear writings revise earlier stage models; the institutional meaning of self-emancipation and transition; and the relation of class to race, gender, colonialism, and ecology. It also preserves disagreement over Engels's editorial and theoretical role without either treating collaboration as identity or invoking a simple betrayal narrative.
