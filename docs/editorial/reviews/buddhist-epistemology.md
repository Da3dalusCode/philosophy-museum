## Method and boundary

The branch replacement reviews every article paragraph and rendered branch fact. It treats Buddhist epistemology as a varied historical family rather than one fixed system. Coverage includes pre-Dignāgan Buddhist and Indian contexts, Vasubandhu as predecessor, Dignāga’s systematization, Dharmakīrti’s revision, perception, inference, public proof, apoha, causal efficacy, justification, reflexive awareness, yogic perception, scripture and authority, Sanskrit/Tibetan/Chinese transmission, Indian rivals, later Tibetan diversity, misconceptions, relevance, and reading paths. ## Corrections and qualifications

- The branch is a varied pramāṇa tradition or method family, not timeless Buddhist consensus.
- Its origin has a long Buddhist prehistory; the 500 CE wall anchor denotes systematization, not invention.
- Dignāga and Dharmakīrti are central but do not exhaust Buddhist accounts of knowledge or reason.
- Perception, inference, language, testimony, justification, and successful action remain distinct.
- Apoha has competing Dignāgan, Dharmakīrtian, Indian critical, and Tibetan interpretations.
- Buddhist logic is interpreted through Indian dialogical proof rather than measured as incomplete Aristotelian or modern formal logic.
- Yogācāra, Madhyamaka, and pramāṇa relations are historically disputed.
- Tibetan and Chinese transmissions follow different textual and institutional routes.
- Contemporary comparisons with classification, AI, or reliabilism are bounded analogies, not identity claims.
