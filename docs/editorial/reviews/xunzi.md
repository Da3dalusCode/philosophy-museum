## Findings and corrections

- The conventional 310–235 BCE chronology and associations with Jixia, Chu, Li Si, and Han Fei are qualified rather than treated as a documented career sequence.
- The thirty-two-chapter *Xunzi* is described as a layered received collection with uneven authorship confidence.
- “Human nature is bad” is not rendered as total depravity, inherited metaphysical sin, universal vicious action, or incapacity for goodness.
- The disagreement with Mencius is analyzed through differing classifications of spontaneous tendencies, moral capacities, deliberate effort, and achieved virtue.
- Xunzi orders and educates desire; he does not advocate annihilating embodied appetite.
- Ritual is a human construction that coordinates scarcity and refines emotion, not mere etiquette or external repression.
- Heaven follows constant patterns but retains classical religious-political resonance; the text is not simply modern secular naturalism.
- Conventional naming is public and purposive without becoming arbitrary private fiat.
- Institutional confidence, hierarchy, and sage authority receive critical treatment, and later links to Legalism do not collapse Xunzi into Han Fei.

## Uncertainties retained

Major questions concern chapter authorship and redaction; the translation and scope of `xing e`, `wei`, `xin`, `li`, and `tian`; what evaluative resources make one cultural construction better than another; how far Xunzi permits criticism of established ritual; and the precise intellectual relationships among Xunzi, Li Si, Han Fei, and early imperial institutions.
