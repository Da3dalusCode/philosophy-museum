## Corrections and qualifications

- Kantianism is a contested reception family, not Kant's doctrine copied unchanged.
- The 1786 date is Reinhold's *Letters* publication landmark, not a precise universal origin.
- Jacobi's objection is influential but not treated as an uncontested reading of Kant.
- Fichte's 1794/95 *Foundation* is distinguished from later revisions of the *Wissenschaftslehre* and Kant's rejection.
- German Idealism revises and contests Kantian limits; whether its figures remain Kantians is a classification dispute.
- Beiser's wider nineteenth-century “lost tradition” is an argued revision, not a hidden consensus.
- Classical Marburg and Southwest Neo-Kantianism is distinguished from broader international and nineteenth-century uses of the label.
- Marx and Nietzsche are critics or later analogues, not doctrinal Kantians; continental and critical-theory inheritances are selective and plural.
- Husserl's inheritance is transformative and Heidegger's Kant reading is controversial; postwar philosophical divergence is not assigned one cause.
- Rawls's justice framework is distinguished from later political liberalism, and “Kantian constructivism” is dated to the 1980 article.
- Constructivist/realist, conceptualist/nonconceptualist, and one-object/two-object disputes remain visible.
- Later science is said to challenge particular Kantian theses, not to make every Kantian project impossible.
- Race, gender, colonialism, and rational-capacity disability disputes are explicit; contemporary technology, medicine, climate, and institutional examples are labeled extensions.
- Generic quick facts were replaced by seven substantive concepts, four sequenced reading steps, and five concrete modern applications with UI-addressable atomic claims.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

Open questions include the membership status of Fichte, Schelling, and Hegel; the proper width of “Neo-Kantianism”; whether Kant is best read as a constructivist; the taxonomy of transcendental idealism; the extent of Kant's racial change; the reconstruction of rational-capacity accounts; and how much analytic, continental, and critical-theory work should count as Kantian rather than merely influenced by Kant. Cohen and Cassirer are central in the prose but lack standalone canonical thinker records, so the existing linked-figure list remains conservative.
