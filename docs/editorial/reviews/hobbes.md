## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- Materialism, psychology, language, and science are treated as parts of the philosophical project, not as disposable prefaces to politics.
- The state of nature is described as an analytical condition, an illustrated historical possibility, and a recurring condition of failed authority—not a confident claim that all humans once lived as isolated individuals in continuous combat.
- Fear is both a driver of insecurity and a rational motive for peace; covenant made under fear is not automatically invalid.
- The reviewed article distinguishes natural right, laws of nature, covenant, authorization, representation, and sovereignty rather than compressing them into “social contract.”
- Sovereign authority is extensive and undivided at the level of final public judgment, but not practically omnipotent or morally infallible.
- Immediate self-preservative liberties and the relation between protection and obligation are retained rather than erased by absolutism.
- Religion is a major part of *Leviathan*. Rival readings of Hobbes’s sincerity are recorded without pretending the text settles them conclusively.
- Gender, family, race, colonial examples, and politically sensitive implications are marked as interpretive risks rather than silently generalized.

No exact quotation is retained in the canonical prose. Publication dates distinguish *De Cive*, the 1651 English *Leviathan*, and later Latin revision where relevant.
