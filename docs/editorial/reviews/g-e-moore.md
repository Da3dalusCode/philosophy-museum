## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- Moore is a major early analytic figure, but clarity and anti-idealist analysis are not credited to him as inventions without predecessors.
- The naturalistic fallacy is tied to Moore’s attempted definitions of good, not equated with every inference from facts to values.
- The open-question argument is presented as influential and contested, not as a decisive proof of non-naturalism.
- Moore’s normative view combines non-naturalist metaethics with ideal consequentialism and organic unities; “intuitionism” alone is insufficient.
- The external-world proof is distinguished from the skeptic’s challenge to whether its premises are known, preserving both Moore’s argument and its contested force.
