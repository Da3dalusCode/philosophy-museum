## Method and boundaries

The complete branch article and rendered quick facts were reviewed against primary texts, current specialist philosophy, global intellectual and political history, full open articles and chapters, institutional full-text studies, and explicitly labeled abstracts or chapter records. Scope included classification, 1883 date anchor, origin, chronology, concepts, figures, works, debates, outcomes, misconceptions, contemporary applications, reading paths, and the directly affected branch summary. The 32-section flagship structure and broad global scope were preserved. This review does not make every Marxist thinker, state, national party, uprising, or linked branch independently `claim-reviewed`.

The word count includes canonical article-section paragraph prose only.

## Corrections and qualifications

- Marxism is a contested reception family of theories, movements, organizations, and state projects, not one doctrine; 1883 is an Atlas post-Marx anchor, not a single origin.
- Marx and Engels's collaboration, editing, and mediation replace a pure-doctrine/betrayal story.
- Second International reform and revolutionary currents, Bernstein, Luxemburg, and Lenin are represented as disputes rather than a linear succession.
- Second International, wartime fracture, council-democratic opposition, anticolonial, feminist, and Black Marxist claims were narrowed to what the opened full texts and specialist sources support; record-only metadata no longer carries substantive claims.
- Russian Revolution wording distinguishes mass participation, war, land seizures, competing socialist projects, political centralization, and repression; emergency explains but does not excuse institutions.
- Comintern prose was rewritten around full-text evidence: early transregional networks and uneven centralization are distinguished from later Soviet direction, Popular Front policy, the Nazi–Soviet pact, the 1941 reversal, resistance, and 1943 dissolution.
- Soviet modernization, education, victory, repression, famine, terror, and one-party rule are not reduced to a moral ledger or a single causal theory.
- Postwar Eastern Europe now uses page-specific evidence for Soviet predominance, Hungary in 1956, and Czechoslovakia in 1968. Unsupported four-country and welfare balance-sheet lists were removed.
- Eurocommunism is narrowed to the Italian project, Western coordination, constitutional pluralism, distance from Moscow, and documented limits; unsupported municipal, union, and anticolonial detail was removed.
- Gramsci's notebooks are fragmentary, revisional, censored, and editorially constructed; Benjamin is an interlocutor, not falsely made Institute staff.
- Maoist theory is distinguished from later labels and state outcomes. Great Leap expectations are “unrealistic,” famine and Cultural Revolution harms are explicit, and numerical or monocausal certainty is rejected.
- Anticolonial, Black, feminist, workerist, analytical, religious, and ecological Marxisms are differentiated rather than treated as decorative additions or one program.
- Structured major-figure links were restricted to canonical records with direct support; Sartre and Beauvoir remain relevant neighboring interlocutors but are not encoded as Marxism's reviewed representative figures.
- Political-economy, religion, and contemporary-analysis passages now identify research programs and extensions without presenting broad applications as settled historical results.
- Generic quick facts were replaced by eight substantive concepts, four sequenced reading steps, and six concrete applications with UI-addressable atomic claims.

## Disputes and unresolved questions

The page leaves open the boundary of Marxism; the relative causal weight of doctrine, organization, war, leaders, institutions, and local conditions in state outcomes; reform versus rupture; party leadership versus self-emancipation; planning, markets, law, pluralism, and opposition; class reduction and the autonomy or co-constitution of race, gender, nation, caste, colonialism, disability, and sexuality; productivism versus ecological reconstruction; and the definition and scale of repression and famine. Those disputes are reasons for precise classification, not reasons to withhold judgment about documented coercion.
