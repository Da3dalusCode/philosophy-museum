## Corrections, qualifications, and disputes

- The Second Discourse is conjectural history, not current anthropology or a literal return-to-nature program.
- Amour-propre is socially comparative and potentially redirectable, not a synonym for all self-love.
- The general will is distinguished from majority preference, private aggregation, executive government, and a ruler’s claim to know a hidden true will.
- Sovereignty and government, natural/civil/moral freedom, and law/decree are separated.
- The *Discourse on Inequality*, *Social Contract*, *Émile*, *Julie*, and autobiographical works retain different genres and projects.
- Civil religion’s toleration and coercive boundary remain in tension.
- Émile’s autonomy is cultivated through concealed control; Sophie’s dependence is a central limitation, not an appendix.
- Rousseau’s educational authority is held against the documented abandonment of his children without using biography as a substitute for argument.
- Music, language, theater, botany, nature, autobiography, Romanticism, democratic reception, and authoritarian appropriation are included without forcing a unified system.
