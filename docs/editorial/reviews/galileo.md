## Corrections and uncertainties retained

- Galileo did not invent the telescope or work through one universal scientific method.
- Instrument-mediated seeing requires construction, calibration, practice, repetition, drawing, and inference.
- Telescopic evidence damaged Ptolemaic astronomy but did not alone eliminate the Tychonic alternative.
- The tide argument was wrong and cannot be retroactively made into proof of Earth’s motion.
- Galilean relativity and inertia are not silently replaced by the complete Newtonian forms.
- The 1616 admonition, disputed stronger injunction, licensing, and 1633 procedure remain distinct.
- Condemnation and coerced abjuration are not minimized, but physical torture, lifelong dungeon confinement, execution, and “and yet it moves” are rejected as unsupported myths.
- “Science versus religion” is treated as inadequate without denying coercive institutional injustice.
