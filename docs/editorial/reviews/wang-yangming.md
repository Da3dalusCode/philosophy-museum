## Claims retained

The review retains Wang Shouren’s secure 1472–1529 lifespan and conventional name Yangming; his roles as Ming official, commander, and teacher; Longchang as the retrospective turning point of his mature teaching; heart-mind as pattern; relational “one body” language; `liangzhi` as pure or innate knowing; unity of knowing and acting; rectifying affairs in his reinterpretation of the *Great Learning*; cultivation amid ordinary responsibilities; and the late Four Sentences as a major but disputed node in his reception.

## Corrections and qualifications

- “Neo-Confucian,” “school of mind,” and Lu–Wang are later organizing labels. They identify meaningful comparisons without proving one unbroken institution or a complete self-description.
- Wang’s mind-as-pattern account is relational and does not make the private ego create or contain the external world.
- `Liangzhi` names an innate moral capacity, not a conscious rulebook present at birth and not the infallibility of sincere personal feeling.
- Unity of knowing and acting concerns ethically engaged knowledge. It is not an automatic causal rule for every known fact, nor does it eliminate deliberation and weakness-of-will questions.
- Wang’s reinterpretation of `gewu` as rectifying affairs criticizes a version of external accumulation from within Confucian learning. The bamboo story is a later pedagogical account and not a sufficient description of Zhu Xi’s method.
- Quiet-sitting can assist a learner but is not the exclusive path; intentions must be examined and corrected in family, office, conflict, and routine.
- The Four Sentences are paraphrased rather than presented in an uncited English translation. Their 1527 Tianquan association, textual record, and relation to Qian Dehong’s “four haves” and Wang Ji’s “four nothings” remain interpretively contested.
- Wang’s campaigns and suppression of rebellions are coercive state actions that complicate the heroic image of decisive moral practice.
- Later Chinese, Japanese, and Korean receptions are diverse. The profile rejects simple causal lines from Wang’s philosophy to every later activist, reformist, or nationalist appropriation.

## Translation, dispute, and unresolved questions

The article uses pure knowing, innate knowing, and innate moral knowledge to mark the range of `liangzhi` without pretending that one gloss is exhaustive. It distinguishes the heart-mind’s original capacity from the correction of actual intentions. Unresolved questions include how moral confidence is checked when disagreement persists; whether unity of knowing and acting is a definition, cultivation strategy, or moral-psychological thesis; how “one body” bears on political hierarchy; and whether the Four Sentences coordinate sudden realization and gradual effort or preserve a deeper inconsistency.

The profile treats Buddhist resemblance as a question for specific comparison. Wang’s criticism of withdrawal and commitment to Confucian relationships do not erase centuries of Buddhist conceptual influence, while shared vocabulary does not make him a disguised Chan teacher.
