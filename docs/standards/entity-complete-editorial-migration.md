# Entity-complete editorial migration

The normal unit of editorial migration is one **entity-complete canonical pass**. A philosopher, philosophy, branch, school, tradition, movement, method, or framework should not be migrated through disconnected article-depth, accuracy, evidence, and Museum-cleanup batches when those surfaces represent the same entity.

## Required inventory

Before release, an entity-complete pass must inspect every applicable claim-bearing or authoritative surface for the target:

- canonical article depth, structure, prose claims, and sources;
- structured facts, terminology, classification, chronology, and date confidence;
- paragraph and structured-claim evidence mappings, formal review note, and current review lock;
- primary Museum interpretation and claim-bearing supplemental Museum surfaces;
- object and image identification, provenance, rights, limitations, and accessibility text;
- search, directory, comparison, timeline, Big History, Philosophy Map, learning-path, and related-entity representations;
- Museum assignment, route, room, and fixed-contract implications; and
- generated manifests and authoritative mirrors.

The pass changes only surfaces that are materially incorrect, stale, shallow, broken, or inconsistent with the reviewed canonical record. Correct content is inspected and preserved rather than rewritten merely to touch every file.

## Release boundary

Article depth, evidence review, structured metadata, primary Museum reconciliation, and target-specific claim-bearing cleanup belong to the same release. A formal article review must state whether Museum copy was inside its review boundary or reconciled separately. Generated artifacts are refreshed only through their authoritative compiler or reporter.

Entity-complete work does not silently reopen the flagship roster, Museum architecture, canonical assignment, room density, or fixed-count contracts. If reconciliation reveals a substantive unresolved article, evidence, assignment, asset, UI, schema, or contract defect, stop the release and report the blocker under the standards conflict or validation rules.
